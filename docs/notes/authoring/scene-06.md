From `daniel-1.refs.json`: **The ten-day test**

The dining hall over ten days: one table piled with the king's rich food, the
four Judeans' table with vegetables and water. The attendant assigned to them

## Story characters

### Daniel

- Ten days. Then look at us and judge for yourself. That's all we're asking.
- I didn't pick a fight. I proposed a test. There's a difference, and the
  attendant knew it.

### Hananiah

- Day six. Honestly? I don't even miss it.
- The others keep waiting for us to break. It's become a kind of dinner
  theater.

### Mishael

- It's not about the food. It never was. It's about who we trust to keep us
  standing.
- Water tastes better when you know why you're drinking it.

### Azariah

- The attendant keeps checking our faces like we might faint mid-bite. We
  won't.
- Ten days is nothing. Our people ate manna for forty years.

### The attendant

- Ten days. If this goes wrong, remember, it was your idea, not mine.
- I don't understand it. The ones eating roast and wine look tired, and the
  vegetable table is thriving. Explain that to me.

## NPCs

- **Cook**: Four plates of vegetables again? You people are ruining my
  reputation.
- **Trainee at the rich table**: More for the rest of us, I say.

## Encounter: DEU.8.2-4 (Torah (Gen-Deut))

- **Anchor:** DAN.1.12
- **Guide persona:** the Elder
- **Scripture cards:** open at the start of this encounter: DAN.1.12 (anchor)
  and DEU.8.2-4; both must be read before the six insight cards unlock.
- **Curated note (from refs.json, already final):** During Israel's years in the wilderness, God deliberately let them go hungry and fed them only manna, even keeping their clothes and bodies from wearing out, all to teach them that real life comes from depending on God, not from a full, rich plate. That is the principle underneath Daniel's test: trusting God matters more than the king's menu.
