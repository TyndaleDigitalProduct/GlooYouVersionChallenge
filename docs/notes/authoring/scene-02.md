crates of gold and silver vessels down the steps in a slow, orderly line while

## Story characters

### Daniel

- They can carry the cups to Babylon. They can't carry God there; he's
  already everywhere they're going.
- Watch the soldiers' faces. Even they handle those vessels carefully.

### Hananiah

- I keep waiting for the sky to break open. It just stays quiet.
- We're on the list. All four of us. They're taking us with the treasure.

### Mishael

- My uncle served in that Temple his whole life. I can't even look at him
  today.
- "The Lord gave." That's what the scroll says. I'm still learning how to
  hear that.

### Azariah

- Jeremiah said seventy years. That means there's an *end*, you know? I'm
  holding on to the end.
- Pack light, they told us. As if anything we own is the heavy part.

## NPCs

- **Babylonian soldier**: Keep the line moving. These crates don't carry
  themselves.
- **Onlooker in the crowd**: First the gold. What do they take next?

## Encounter: PSA.106.40-42 (OT Poetry/Wisdom)

- **Anchor:** DAN.1.2
- **Guide persona:** Lady Wisdom
- **Scripture cards:** open at the start of this encounter: DAN.1.2 (anchor)
  and PSA.106.40-42; both must be read before the six insight cards unlock.
- **Curated note (from refs.json, already final):** A recap of Israel's history: God let hostile nations conquer them because they kept turning away from him, the same 'God allowed this' idea behind Daniel 1:2.

### Cards — six per encounter, see README for the rules

| Value | Card text |
| ----- | --------- |
| 5     | The psalm shows "the Lord gave" in Daniel 1:2 is a pattern, not a one-off: God has handed his people to their enemies before, whenever they kept turning away. |
| 4     | What looks like Babylon's victory in Daniel 1:2 is, in the psalm's words, God's own anger at work; the conqueror isn't the real actor. |
| 3     | The psalm's phrase, God "handed them over" to the nations, is the same move Daniel 1:2 describes: the treasures given into Babylon's hand. |
| 3     | The psalm's long cycle of rebellion and defeat is the backstory that makes sense of the Temple treasures leaving Jerusalem. |
| 0     | The psalm proves Daniel 1:2 wrong: God's people had never been conquered before Babylon came. |
| 0     | The psalm says God handed his people over even though they had stayed faithful to him. |
